Problem Lab

Team roles

Four roles, rotated every week. Everyone talks, everyone builds.

Leader
Keeps the team on the task.“Our goal now is…”
Timekeeper
Watches the timer.“We have 3 minutes left.”
Recorder
Writes the team's ideas.“Let me write that down.”
Speaker
Shares with the class.“Our team found…”

Spot the innovation

As a team: classify each case. The feedback explains every option.

1. Amazon Kindle: books read on a dedicated e-reader instead of paper.
2. RTA Nol card: one contactless card to pay for metro, bus and tram in Dubai.
3. Häagen-Dazs: ice cream presented as an adult luxury, not a children's treat.
4. Kitopi: one kitchen cooks delivery meals for many restaurant brands.

Inclusive learning · students of determination

Same course, more ways in

Built on the three UDL principles (CAST, UDL Guidelines 3.0). Adjustments for individual students follow CUD's student-support process.

EngagementWhy learn: real problems, team roles, choice.
RepresentationHow to see it: pictures, simple words, read-aloud.
Action & expressionHow to show it: draw, say or write.

01Same idea, simpler

Every key concept has an Easy-view version: short words and a picture chain.

Standard
Innovation: a change that adds value to an existing product or service.
Easy view
Innovation = a change that people like and use.
Idea→People use it→It helps

02Visual schedule

The session plan as pictures, shown at the start, so nothing is a surprise.

Fun factIdeaVideoTeam workShareExit ticket

03Small steps, with a timer

Big tasks are cut into ticks. A visual timer shows time left.

04Choose how to show it

The same learning outcome, three ways to show it. The rubric marks the idea, not the format.

05Teamwork with clear roles

Fixed roles with one-line scripts mean every student knows what to do and say. Roles rotate weekly.

LeaderTimekeeperRecorderSpeaker
