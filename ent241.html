3.Course Details
3.1Course Description

Entrepreneurship 1 is the third course of the CUD entrepreneurship pathway and the first of two Business courses (ENT 241–ENT 242). Building on the problem, evidence and tested MVP from ENT 141 and ENT 142, students analyse a commercial opportunity and plan a venture. They use the Value Proposition Canvas, PESTLE, Porter's Five Forces and SWOT to analyse the opportunity and its environment, compare business model types including franchising and licensing, build and redesign a Business Model Canvas, and plan marketing, capital, legal foundations, intellectual property, operations and risk. The course ends with a business plan reviewed at a Business Plan Clinic and an investor pitch. Each week is a 2-hour class that combines a short lecture with an in-class challenge.

5.Teaching Pedagogy

The course is taught as one 2-hour class each week (2-0-2). The class opens with a short lecture on one concept from the textbook, with UAE examples, and continues with one in-class challenge (Section 4.1) in which teams apply the tool to their own venture. Teams of 4–5 keep one venture from Week 1 to the investor pitch and carry it into ENT 242.

Some classes may include a short contribution from a practising entrepreneur, an investor or a CUD Incubator partner, within the scheduled class time; this does not add contact hours and does not replace the lecture or the in-class challenge.

Students are encouraged to take part in business-plan competitions, hackathons and incubator programmes at CUD and UAE level.

Inclusive learning: Materials follow Universal Design for Learning: templates, short steps and sentence starters for every in-class challenge, and students may show their learning in writing, speaking or visuals. Individual adjustments follow CUD's student-support process.

9.Assessment Methodology

Four assignments of 25%, one after each block of study. Each one is built in the in-class challenges, so students work on it with the instructor present. Teams of 4–5 keep the same venture across ENT 241 and ENT 242; individual marks come from individual questions and peer evaluation.

A1 – Opportunity & environment analysis (Team, Week 5, 25%): For one commercial opportunity: a Value Proposition Canvas built from evidence, a UAE PESTLE with one future scenario, Porter's Five Forces and the SWOT implications. Built in the Week 1–5 challenges; up to 1500 words with the canvases.

A2 – Business model pack (Team, Week 9, 25%): A Business Model Canvas for the venture and one redesigned alternative (for example subscription, platform, franchise or licensing), comparing how each creates and captures value. Built in the Human BMC Workshop and the Business Model Remix.

A3 – Business plan (Team, Week 13, 25%): A business plan of up to 3000 words: opportunity and value proposition, market and industry analysis, business model, marketing and the first 10 customers, capital and funding, legal foundations and IP, operations, risks and pre-mortem, growth and exit options, milestones. Reviewed at the Business Plan Clinic before submission; each member submits a peer evaluation.

A4 – Investor pitch (Team pitch, individual Q&A, Week 15, 25%): A pitch of up to 8 minutes to a panel (the instructor, with external entrepreneurs or investors where possible), followed by individual questions on assumptions, market, model and finance. Panel response: Invest / Need More Evidence / Pass, with feedback that carries into ENT 242.

Note: A student will lose 10% or more for non-compliance with group efforts (teamwork) and failing to meet tutor deadlines. No late project will be accepted. Exceptions for late evaluation are up to the discretion of the instructor.
