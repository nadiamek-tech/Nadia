3.Course Details
3.1Course Description

This course is a continuation of ENT 141 and turns a real problem into a tested solution. Teams take a challenge posted by a CUD partner business on the CUD Incubator's Solve It board, meet its customers, analyse the market with AI tools and verified UAE data, and build and test a minimum viable product (MVP). The course ends with Accelerator Day, where teams pitch to student, partner and incubator investors. Students reflect on how these skills shape their own career paths.

5.Teaching Pedagogy
5.1MVP labs (Weeks 8–11)

Teardown (Week 8): each team studies the first MVP of a CUD Incubator startup: what was built first, what was left out, and what users taught the founders.

Rebuild (Week 9): the team applies the same MVP type to its own challenge.

Test (Weeks 10–11): five real users from the partner; one change is recorded after each test, then the Lean Canvas is completed.

5.Teaching Pedagogy
5.2Customer and market labs (Weeks 2–7)

Customers: interviews at the partner site and a short Microsoft Form survey. Personas are built only from what customers said.

Market analysis with AI: students use AI assistants to find and summarise sources, then check each number against the original source (UAE open data, Dubai Statistics Center, company reports). Rule: no source, no number.

Output: market size, competition matrix, and social and economic impact (CLO 3).

9.Assessment Methodology
A4 – Accelerator Day (Week 14)

Training (Weeks 12–13): pitch structure, storytelling, slides, timing and Q&A drills with mentors and partners.

Investors: every student and guest receives 100,000 CUD Coins as stickers; partners and incubator judges receive 500,000. Nobody may invest in their own team or put more than 40% of their coins in one team.

Prize: the most-funded team receives an incubator fast-track. CUD Coins are not graded; the pitch is graded on the rubric.
