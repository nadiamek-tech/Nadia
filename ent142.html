Solve It @ CUD

New

CUD partners (cafés, shops, companies) post real problems on the incubator's Solve It board. ENT 142 teams pick one and solve it, step by step.

Inspired by Soolvit, an open-innovation platform that turns company challenges into guided hackathons with prizes.

01

Course description

Change

From a sector-wide social venture to one real challenge from a real partner.

Current syllabus
Tap to enlarge
Proposed

ENT 142 turns a real problem into a tested solution. Teams take a challenge posted by a CUD partner business on the incubator's Solve It board, meet its customers, analyse the market with AI tools and verified UAE data, then build and test an MVP.

The course ends at Accelerator Day, where teams pitch to student, partner and incubator investors.

05

MVP: learn from a real one first

New

The MVP is the hardest step, so students first take apart the first MVP of a startup from the incubator, then plan their own.

Current syllabus
Tap to enlarge
Proposed
  • Teardown (Wk 8): what did they build first, what did they leave out, what did they learn from users?
  • Rebuild (Wk 9): the same MVP type applied to the team's own challenge.
  • Test (Wk 10): five real users from the partner, one change after each test.
  • Cases: MVPs supplied by the CUD Incubator.
06

Market and customers

Change

"Market analysis" was one line in the report. It becomes taught and practised: AI tools for speed, primary sources for truth.

Current syllabus
Tap to enlarge
Proposed
  • Customers: interviews at the partner site plus a short Microsoft Form; personas built only from what customers said.
  • AI research lab: students use AI assistants to find and summarise sources, then check each number against the original (UAE open data, Dubai Statistics Center, company reports).
  • Rule: no source, no number.
  • Output: market size, competition matrix and social and economic impact (CLO 3).
07

Pitching: an accelerator simulation

Change

A pitch to the instructor becomes Accelerator Day: proper training first, then real-feeling investors.

Current syllabus
Tap to enlarge
Proposed
  • Training (Wk 12–13): structure, storytelling, slides, timing, and Q&A drills with mentors.
  • Investors: every student and guest gets 100,000 CUD Coins as stickers; partners and incubator judges get 500,000.
  • Rules: no investing in your own team; at most 40% of your coins in one team.
  • Live board: funds raised per team, shown on screen.
  • Prize: the most-funded team gets an incubator fast-track. Coins are not graded.
08

Textbook and readings

Change

Byers stays, because ENT 142 is where its venture content belongs. Practical MVP and testing books are added; the generic international-business journal list goes.

Current syllabus
Tap to enlarge
Proposed
  • Core (kept): Byers, Dorf & Nelson (2019), Technology Ventures, 5th ed.
  • MVP and testing: Bland & Osterwalder (2019), Testing Business Ideas, Wiley
  • CUD reading: Chabani, Askri & Kweh (eds.), Sustainable Innovation in the MENA Region, IGI Global, 2025
  • Data: UAE open data, Dubai Statistics Center, partner data
  • Kept: Liedtka et al. (2017); Traifeh et al. (2021)
