3.Course Details
3.1Course Description

This course is a continuation of ENT 141. Teams turn their ENT 141 prototype, or a CUD Incubator partner challenge, into a minimum viable product (MVP), test it with real users at a live Innovation Exhibition, and analyse the evidence for desirability, feasibility and viability. The course ends with two pitch events to an academic and external panel, scored by peer investors, and a founder learning session that prepares students for ENT 241 (Analyze & Plan). Each week is a 2-hour lab.

5.Teaching Pedagogy

The course is taught as a weekly 2-hour lab (0-2-1). Each lab opens with a short concept from the readings and continues with one hands-on activity (Section 4.1). Teams build and test their own minimum viable product (MVP) and keep a teamwork log with rotating roles.

Some labs may include a short contribution from a practising entrepreneur, a product designer or a CUD Incubator partner, within the scheduled class time; this does not add contact hours and does not replace the lab.

Teams may work on a challenge published by a CUD partner through the CUD Incubator, and are encouraged to enter hackathons, innovation challenges and competitions at CUD and UAE level.

Inclusive learning: Materials follow Universal Design for Learning. An Easy version of every lecture, lab, worksheet and assignment gives short numbered steps, a sentence starter, read-aloud and one part at a time, and students may show their learning by drawing, speaking or writing. Individual adjustments follow CUD's student-support process.

5.Teaching Pedagogy
5.1MVP labs (Weeks 1–4)

Prototype Exhibition (Week 1): teams show their ENT 141 prototypes; peers separate core value from unnecessary features.

Riskiest Assumption (Week 2): what must be true for the venture to work, and what the MVP must test. Teams may study the first MVP of a CUD Incubator startup.

Build Sprint and Break My MVP (Weeks 3–4): hands-on building with founder check-ins, then peer teams stress-test the MVP.

5.Teaching Pedagogy
5.3Pitch training and pitch events (Weeks 13–16)

Training (Week 13): 60-second no-slide pitch, then a pitch carousel to different audiences, improving after every round.

Pitch Events I and II (Weeks 14–15): teams pitch to an academic and external panel; individual questions check understanding.

Peer investor scorecard: students invest CUD Coins in other teams (not their own, at most 40% in one team). Coins are not graded; the pitch is graded on the rubric.

Founder Learning Session (Week 16): what did the market prove us wrong about? The answer feeds the individual reflection.
